A pachinko is a gaming parlor in Korea / Japan. The machine
played in these places is what we call a pinball machine. These parlors
provide the backdrop for this novel. Main characters are employed by
them or manage them. Many are corrupt. They are often one of the few
places Koreans who live in Japan are able to get jobs. Japan had
colonized Korea in this period (the mid 1950s).

Several of the principal characters in the story, Sunja, her mother ..,
her brother-in-law Yoseb and a businessman named Hansu benefit from
these places. Hansu is corrupt and brilliant. He meets Sunja after she
moves from Korea to Japan to live with Isak and his brother Yoseb. (Isak
had showed up earlier at Sunja's home in Korea. He was a priest and was
very sick.) Sunja becomes pregnant by Hansu.

We learn all this in the early chapters of this novel. Min Jin Lee's
epic covers 5 generations. This is historical fiction at its best and
will resonate with today's American readers who were born, as I was, in
the 40s. After reading this masterpiece I now understand a lot more
about the Korean conflict, the division of Korea into North and South
and the fallout of WWII. (Pyongyang and Nagasaki are mentioned in this
book about midway thru. Yoseb ends up in these towns to take a job in a
steel mill. He lives, but comes home badly injured and will be cared for
by his family for many years. The scary rhetoric coming out of current
day Pyongyang is what I thought of when this town was mentioned.)
