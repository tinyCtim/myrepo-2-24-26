It took me a while to finish. Donna Tartt's excellent writing requires
close attention. I picked up The Little Friend at my library's book sale
and have just purchased her very successful first novel The Secret
History. I've read The Goldfinch. The author takes many years to write
her books. I wonder what's next.

Harriet is a 12-year-old girl living in Mississippi in the 1970s. Very
early in the novel we learn her 9-year-old brother Robin is dead by a
hanging.

The large Ratliff family is involved in a meth lab and have been in and
out of prison for various crimes. Danny Ratliff becomes the prime
suspect in Harriet's mind for the murder of her brother.

Harriet's mother Charlotte and sister Allison become despondent and
spend a lot of time letting things go. Harriet and her friend Hely
decide to go after Danny and cook up a scheme involving snakes.

Ida is a maid in Charlotte's house. She is loved by Harriet. A very
touching scene happens when she is let go by Charlotte after many years
of service. Harriet's grandmother Edie and four great aunts play a large
role in the novel. They are sometimes helpful to Harriet but mostly
provide an environment Harriet would rather not be in.

There are several fairly tense and scary chapters. In one, Farshed
Ratliff, Danny's older brother, is challenged to a pool game. In
another, born again preacher Eugene Ratliff is in a Mission building.
Harriet has released a large number of poisonous snakes. Later Harriet
smashes Farshed's car.

As the story nears its end things get very dark. If you're looking for a
clear resolution to the murder this is not your book. But if you want to
see how both the poor and the not poor think, live, and interact, I
recommend this book. It's long (555 pages). Stick with it.
